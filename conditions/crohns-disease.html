Crohn's Disease

A type of inflammatory bowel disease that can cause inflammation anywhere in the digestive tract — most often the end of the small intestine.

What it is

Crohn's disease is a long-term form of inflammatory bowel disease. The immune system drives inflammation that can appear anywhere along the gut, from the mouth to the anus, though it most commonly affects the last part of the small intestine (the terminal ileum) and the beginning of the colon.

A key feature is that inflammation occurs in patches, with healthy stretches of bowel in between, and it can reach through the full thickness of the bowel wall. Over time this can lead to narrowing (strictures) or tunnels between loops of bowel (fistulas).

Crohn's vs colitis: Crohn's can affect any part of the gut in patches and through the whole wall; ulcerative colitis affects only the colon and rectum, in a continuous pattern and only the lining.

Symptoms

  • Tummy pain and cramping, often in the lower right side
  • Persistent or recurring diarrhoea
  • Fatigue and feeling generally unwell
  • Unintended weight loss
  • Blood or mucus in the stool
  • Mouth ulcers, and soreness around the anus

Causes & risk factors

As with other forms of IBD, Crohn's results from an interaction between genes, the immune system, gut bacteria and the environment. A family history raises the risk, it is often diagnosed in younger adults, and smoking makes Crohn's more likely and tends to worsen its course.

Diagnosis

  • Blood and stool tests, including faecal calprotectin
  • Colonoscopy with biopsies
  • MRI or CT scans to assess the small bowel and look for complications
  • Capsule endoscopy in some cases

Treatment & management

There is no cure, but treatment can induce and maintain remission and prevent complications.

  • Steroids or liquid nutrition therapy to settle a flare
  • Immunosuppressants and biologic therapies to maintain remission
  • Surgery to remove or repair badly affected sections — common in Crohn's, though disease can recur
  • Stopping smoking, which meaningfully improves outcomes

Living with it

With modern treatment and regular specialist review, most people with Crohn's can manage their symptoms well. Good nutrition, stopping smoking, monitoring for flare warning signs and support for mental wellbeing are all important parts of care.
