Ulcerative Colitis

What it is

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a long-term form of inflammatory bowel disease. The immune system causes the inner lining of the large bowel to become inflamed, developing tiny ulcers that can bleed and produce mucus.

UC always involves the rectum and extends a variable distance up the colon in a continuous pattern. Unlike Crohn's disease — which can affect the whole gut in patches — UC is limited to the colon and rectum.

Symptoms

  • Diarrhoea, often containing blood or mucus
  • An urgent and frequent need to empty the bowel
  • Tummy pain and cramping, often eased by passing a stool
  • Feeling of needing to go even when the bowel is empty (tenesmus)
  • Tiredness, weight loss and a reduced appetite during flares
Seek urgent help if you have a severe flare with frequent bloody stools, fever, a racing heart or significant tummy pain.

Causes & risk factors

UC develops from a combination of genetic, immune and environmental factors. Risk is higher with a family history of IBD, and it is most common in people of working age. Interestingly, UC is more common in non-smokers and former smokers.

Diagnosis

  • Blood tests for inflammation and anaemia
  • Stool tests, including faecal calprotectin, and tests to rule out infection
  • Colonoscopy with biopsies to confirm the diagnosis and map how much of the colon is involved

Treatment & management

The aim is to bring flares under control and then keep the bowel healed and in remission.

  • Aminosalicylates (5-ASAs) — often the first-line treatment, taken by mouth or as an enema/suppository
  • Steroids — short courses to settle a flare
  • Immunosuppressants and biologic therapies — for more active or hard-to-treat disease
  • Surgery — removing the colon can be curative for UC and is an option for some people

Living with it

Many people with UC lead full, active lives. Taking maintenance treatment as prescribed, attending reviews, knowing your personal flare warning signs and looking after sleep, stress and diet all help keep the condition stable.
