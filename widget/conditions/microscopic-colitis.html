Microscopic Colitis

Inflammation of the colon that can only be seen under a microscope, causing chronic, watery, non-bloody diarrhoea.

What it is

Microscopic colitis is a type of inflammatory bowel disease affecting the large bowel. Its defining feature is that the colon usually looks completely normal during a colonoscopy — the inflammation is only visible when a tissue sample (biopsy) is examined under a microscope.

Because the bowel looks normal, the condition is often mistaken for irritable bowel syndrome. It is a frequent and treatable cause of ongoing watery diarrhoea, particularly in older adults.

Subtypes

Collagenous colitis

A thickened band of collagen forms just beneath the surface lining of the colon.

Lymphocytic colitis

An increased number of white blood cells (lymphocytes) is seen in the colon lining.

Same treatment

The two subtypes cause similar symptoms and are generally managed in the same way.

Symptoms

  • Chronic watery diarrhoea that is not bloody
  • An urgent need to empty the bowel, sometimes at night
  • Tummy pain or cramps
  • Faecal incontinence in some people
  • Tiredness and mild weight loss

Causes & risk factors

The cause isn't fully known. It is more common in women and in people over 60, and is linked with autoimmune conditions such as coeliac disease, thyroid disorders and rheumatoid arthritis. Some medicines and smoking can also increase the risk.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis relies on a colonoscopy with biopsies, even when the bowel looks normal — the laboratory findings confirm the type. Blood and stool tests help exclude other causes such as coeliac disease or infection.

Why it's missed: a normal-looking colonoscopy can falsely reassure. In anyone with persistent watery diarrhoea, biopsies are essential to avoid a missed diagnosis.

Treatment & management

  • Budesonide, a targeted steroid, is usually the first-line treatment and works well for most people
  • Reviewing and stopping any medicines that may be contributing
  • Anti-diarrhoeal medicines for milder symptoms
  • Stopping smoking and simple dietary adjustments

Symptoms often settle with treatment, though they can return and may need a further course.
